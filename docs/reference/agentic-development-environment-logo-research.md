# Agentic development environment logo research

Research date: 2026-09-09. Scope: current agentic development environments (ADEs), coding-agent
workbenches, and adjacent coding agents whose brands appear next to AgentStart in product UI.

## Decision

AgentStart should not look like another AI model, a single coding agent, or a conventional IDE. Its
strongest ownable idea is **heterogeneous specialists converging into one finished result**: Codex can
operate the computer or generate imagery, Claude can own a difficult implementation, Gemini can shape
language, and other agents can contribute their own strengths while AgentStart coordinates the work.

The recommended direction is a compact **convergence mark**:

- Three visibly different inputs approach one shared center. Their endpoints or lane shapes differ
  subtly, communicating unlike providers, skills, and output types rather than cloned workers.
- One stronger form leaves the center as a resolved artifact or forward motion. The visual sentence is
  “many specialists in, one piece of work out,” not merely “start.”
- The outer silhouette may hint at an `A`, but it must read first as a collaboration mark. Do not use an
  `AS` monogram; it says nothing about the product and is awkward in English.
- The canonical mark is flat and works in one color. A full-size application icon may add one restrained
  signal color, but recognition must not depend on a gradient or on three provider-like brand colors.

This occupies a useful gap. Most peers visualize a single object, initial, mascot, editor, or parent
company. Devin comes closest to “many nodes,” but its small black node cluster does not communicate
different specialties becoming a shared deliverable. Codex and Claude Code have multi-agent products,
but their public product pages still lean on the parent brand rather than a distinct collaboration mark.

## Method and interpretation

The sources below are first-party brand pages, product pages, product announcements, or official
repositories. Statements such as “preferred lockup” and “scales well” are attributed to the owner.
Descriptions of visual form and small-size performance are this research's assessment of the official
artwork, not claims made by the companies unless explicitly noted.

“Small” means the use cases AgentStart actually has to survive: a 16 px Chrome toolbar/favicon, a 24 px
menu-bar template image, provider grids, and native app icons. Marketing art that only works at hero size
does not count as a durable mark.

## What the category has converged on

1. **Simple geometry beats literal AI imagery.** Cursor, Windsurf, Zed, Replit, Devin, Junie, and
   Antigravity all reduce the identity to a cube, path, frame, blocks, nodes, or an initial. A robot head,
   brain, terminal window, magic wand, and sparkle would make AgentStart look less mature and less
   ownable.
2. **The semantic core must survive without material effects.** Most brands retain a black/white symbol
   or a high-contrast base. Cursor and Codex use dimensional app artwork, but their cube silhouette and
   terminal prompt still do the recognition work after tiny highlights disappear.
3. **Initials are crowded.** Windsurf, Zed, Junie, and Antigravity all bend an initial into geometry.
   Another stylized `A` would enter the busiest part of the category and reduce the collaboration story to
   the product's first letter.
4. **A symbol and a wordmark have different jobs.** Marketing often prefers the horizontal lockup;
   constrained UI uses a standalone symbol. Cursor even maintains separate dimensional treatments for
   logo, app icon, and avatar.
5. **Parent-brand equity can replace some product distinctiveness.** Copilot, Claude Code, and the Codex
   marketing site can lean on GitHub, Claude, or OpenAI even when their constrained app contexts use a
   product icon. AgentStart cannot borrow that shortcut: its symbol must become recognizable on its own.
6. **Very few marks explain multi-agent collaboration.** Devin suggests a network, but its nodes are
   identical. The rest communicate one editor, one agent, one initial, or one parent brand. None clearly
   says that different specialist agents and modalities are composed into one outcome.

## The positioning gap AgentStart can own

AgentStart's identity is not “an IDE with AI” and not “the agent that does everything.” It is the neutral
collaboration layer where unlike agents, providers, tools, and output types can contribute without losing
their individual advantages. This changes the logo brief in four ways:

- **Heterogeneity must be visible.** Repeating three identical dots only says parallelism or a network.
  Inputs need a controlled difference in geometry, direction, or terminal shape.
- **Coordination needs an outcome.** An orbit or mesh says activity but not completion. The mark needs a
  clear resolved output, shared center, or directional exit.
- **AgentStart must remain visually neutral beside provider brands.** The interface already places
  Claude, Codex, Gemini, Cursor, and other marks together. A rainbow identity would compete with them and
  could imply that AgentStart is another model provider. The product brand should frame the ecosystem.
- **“Start” is energy, not the whole concept.** Forward motion can be present in the output stroke, but a
  play button or rocket would misdescribe AgentStart as a launcher and erase ongoing orchestration,
  review, evidence, and handoff.

## Recommended identity brief

### Primary concept: convergence

Construct the mark from three short, bold approaches with unlike terminal geometries—for example round,
square, and chamfered—that share one junction. From the junction, one wider path resolves into a simple
artifact/forward form. Keep the form open enough that it does not become a knot at 16 px.

The desired readings, in order, are:

1. different specialists working together;
2. coordinated work becoming one deliverable;
3. forward momentum;
4. a subtle `A`, only if it emerges without forcing the drawing.

The strongest animation extension is also natural: inputs arrive with different timing, align at the
junction, and the completed form exits once. The static final frame remains the real logo; motion is not
required to understand it.

### Shape and color

- Use a filled or heavy-stroke silhouette with no more than four primary masses.
- Make black and white the authoritative versions. They must be identical in geometry and work as a
  macOS menu-bar template image.
- Use near-black/soft-white as the product foundation. If an accent is wanted, test one signal color on
  the junction or app-icon field rather than coloring every input. A warm yellow or amber is worth
  exploring because it reads as activation while staying separate from the many blue, violet, and mint
  AI brands; it must not drift into warning-state semantics in the product UI.
- Avoid essential transparency, glow, glass, shadows, and fine gradients. Those can exist in campaign
  art or a 1024 px icon treatment, never as the recognition mechanism.
- Pair the symbol with a straightforward custom-drawn `AgentStart` wordmark. Do not over-tech the type;
  the unusual idea should live in the symbol.

### Directions to reject early

- A literal rocket, play triangle, power button, or terminal prompt: all over-index on “start.”
- A robot, face, brain, or sparkle: they imply a single AI agent and are already category clichés.
- A plain hub-and-spoke or four identical connected dots: too close to generic integrations and Devin's
  current visual territory; it does not convey specialist differences or an outcome.
- Three interlocking ribbons or a dense knot: they can express collaboration but become OpenAI-like or
  illegible in toolbar use.
- A cube, nested editor frame, or angle-bracket monogram: Cursor, Zed, and the developer-tool category
  already own this vocabulary.
- Provider-colored rainbow inputs: too Google/Gemini-adjacent and visually noisy next to real provider
  icons.

## Required logo system and acceptance checks

Deliver one coherent system, not one 1024 px illustration:

- standalone logomark;
- horizontal `mark + AgentStart` lockup;
- square app icon for macOS/iOS;
- Chrome icons at 16, 32, 48, and 128 px;
- favicon;
- one-color macOS menu-bar template asset;
- light and dark artwork without changing the silhouette.

Before selecting a direction, compare every candidate in these conditions:

1. Downsample to 16 px and view at 100%, not enlarged.
2. Convert to one-color black, one-color white, and grayscale.
3. Place it beside the official Claude, Codex/OpenAI, Gemini/Antigravity, Cursor, and Copilot marks. It
   must look like the coordinator, not another provider.
4. Test on a browser toolbar, tab favicon, macOS menu bar, iOS home screen, GitHub README, and a dense
   agent/provider picker.
5. Blur slightly or view from a distance. The convergence silhouette and single output should remain;
   terminal-shape differences may simplify but must not turn into visual noise.
6. Ask viewers what the mark suggests without showing the name. “Collaboration,” “convergence,” or
   “different things becoming one” are good; “crypto,” “share,” “Google Drive,” “robot,” and “play button”
   are rejection signals.

asset from it. This research does not replace or regenerate those files; it defines the brief the artwork
should satisfy.
